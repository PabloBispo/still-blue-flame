Where the fire data stops

NASA burned 2,299 samples in microgravity. None of the solid ones in the air a Moon base will breathe. We unified the data, measured that gap, and show what the existing evidence says.

The problem

Fire safety for exploration rests on data nobody can read side by side

  • Future habitats plan an exploration atmosphere of 34% O₂ at 8.2 psia (56.5 kPa). The challenge statement names 30% O₂ at 10.2 psia.
  • More oxygen fraction and less total pressure change how materials burn.
  • NASA's PSI repository holds 24 combustion investigations, but their tables use different columns, units and vocabularies.
  • For solid fuels, the tables list settings, not outcomes. What happened is in logs and videos.
ISS cabin21% O₂ · 101.3 kPa

Question we ask the data: what has actually been burned near these atmospheres, and what has not?

The gap

No solid material was tested near an exploration atmosphere

0 of 261
solid-fuel tests at 30% O₂ or more
  • Highest O₂ in any solid-fuel test: 22.7%.
  • Solid-fuel tests at 70 kPa or less: 0.
  • High-oxygen data exists only for gas flames and droplets.

In O₂ partial pressure the solids do cover the targets (19.2 to 21.3 kPa). The gap is in oxygen fraction and total pressure.

What the evidence says

Below 30% O₂, spherical gas flames rarely sustain. And the videos hold the missing outcomes

  • ACME CFI-G (label by the PI): below 30% O₂, 0 of 64 sustained; at 30% or more, 31 of 115.
  • ACME Flame Design (label from script log): below 30%, 3 of 58; at 30% or more, 86 of 119.
  • Video to data: a deterministic reader (no language model, no generic OCR) turned the telemetry burned into 86 BASS-II test videos (1.2 million frames) into data. It shows how each test ended: 63 flames went out at a steady fan setting, 9 were blown out by the operator, 10 recordings stop first, 4 show no radiometer fall. On a subset of 8 tests, video and spreadsheet agree on air speed at extinction in 7 (median difference 0.13 cm/s).

Gas flames on a spherical burner, not habitat materials. Descriptive counts, no statistical test.

Limits and the next test

What we do not claim, and the experiment that is missing

  • No fitted flammability or quenching boundary. Calibration resolution does not support one.
  • Nothing about partial gravity: PSI holds zero such tests.
  • 97 of 244 BASS-II video files read; 86 tests accepted, readings checked by eye on 5 videos. The camera alone detects extinction reliably in only 35 of 86. The flame mask illustrates the method; it is not a validated measurement.
  • Both extinction readings share one calibration curve, so their agreement is not fully independent.
The missing test

A solid material burned at 30% to 34% O₂ and 57 to 70 kPa.

Next steps: read the remaining BASS-II videos; statistical tests on the gas-flame pattern; an SI agent that answers only from the dataset, with citations.
