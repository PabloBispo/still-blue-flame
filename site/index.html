NASA Space Apps 2026 · Flame in Freefall

NASA burned samples in microgravity. None of the solid ones in the air a Moon base will breathe.

We unified the combustion tables of NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) repository into one citable dataset, measured where the evidence stops, and show what the existing evidence says about fire safety in exploration atmospheres.

Counts from the unified dataset (build of Nov 15, 2026). Every row keeps its PSI ID, DOI, source file, sheet and row.

    02 · What the evidence says

    Below 30% oxygen, spherical gas flames rarely sustain

    Two ISS investigations with different fuels point the same way. Counts of normal (fuel into oxidizer) flames by chamber oxygen band.

    ACME CFI-G label by the PI

    ACME Flame Design label from script log

    Scope: these are gas flames on a spherical burner, not habitat materials. They speak to flame sustainment at high oxygen, not to the flammability of a fabric or a plastic. Descriptive counts, no statistical test.
    New labels: for 552 tests with no outcome in the published tables, we recovered one from the experiment script logs. Checked against the PI's own table in CFI-G, the rule agrees in 269 of 284 tests (94.7%). It cannot tell failed ignition from self-extinction, so it is reported as "not sustained".

    03 · Video to data

    The outcome of solid-fuel tests is in the video, so we read the video

    BASS-II tables list materials and settings, not what happened. In each test the crew stepped the fan down until the flame went out, and the video overlay shows fan, air speed and radiometer. We read that overlay frame by frame and joined it to the test matrix.

    Telemetry read from the overlay

    Fan display (instrument units) and radiometer per second. Dashed line: start of the final radiometer decay.

    Extinction evidence in BASS-II, by source

    Points only. Calibration resolution does not support a fitted quenching boundary.

    What holds and what does not

    • Spreadsheet and video agree on air speed at extinction in 7 of 8 tests (median absolute difference 0.13 cm/s). Both use the same calibration curve.
    • 11 PMMA rods: radiometer decay starts between 0.75 and 2.25 cm/s, at 14.8% to 22.2% O₂. Read as "below about 2 cm/s". No trend with O₂ at this resolution.
    • The video adds data the matrix lacks: tests O3b and P1 have no fan reading in the spreadsheet.
    • Rejected: the camera as an extinction detector (reliable in 4 of 16 videos).
    • Coverage: 16 of 244 BASS-II videos (6.6%).

    Method and limits

    How this was built, and what it does not claim

    Dataset

    • 32 curated column maps, one per source table. No language model in the data path.
    • SI units, original unit kept. Nothing imputed or corrected; suspect values are flagged.
    • 99.7% of cells filled where the source reports the field (4,702 of 4,714).
    • Deterministic pipeline, 39 automated tests.

    Not claimed

    • No fitted flammability or quenching boundary.
    • Nothing about partial gravity: PSI holds zero such tests.
    • No diluent effect in FLEX (groups of 9 and 3).
    • No statistical test or confidence interval behind the counts shown.

    The test that is missing

    A solid material burned at 30% to 34% oxygen and 57 to 70 kPa. Until then, exploration-atmosphere fire safety for materials rests on extrapolation.
